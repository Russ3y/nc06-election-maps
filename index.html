Executive summary

The story behind the result

McDowell's strongest county percentages came from Davie, Davidson and Rowan. But the geographic distribution of votes mattered: Guilford contributed the largest share of districtwide turnout and was McDowell's weakest county by vote percentage. The county results show the contrast between winning a county decisively and accumulating enough votes districtwide.

01 / THE BIGGEST COUNTY

Guilford led the district in total votes.

62,719 votes, or 22.9% of the district total. McDowell received 27.8% of Guilford's recorded vote.

02 / THE REPUBLICAN BASE

Davidson delivered the most McDowell votes.

39,479 McDowell votes and a 65.1% countywide share, despite ranking second in total votes cast.

03 / THE MAP LIMITATION

Precinct color does not capture all voting methods.

Some counties report early and absentee ballots separately. Those ballots cannot safely be assigned to precinct polygons from this dataset.

THE EARLY VOTE

Jefferson led before Election Day.

Jefferson received 93,175 early votes to McDowell's 74,834, a margin of 18,341 votes.

THE MAIL VOTE

Jefferson also led among mail voters.

Jefferson received 4,322 absentee-by-mail votes to McDowell's 2,016, a margin of 2,306.

THE 2024 COMPARISON

Different electorate, different opponent.

McDowell's early-vote share fell from 70.2% in 2024 to 44.5% in 2026. This is a descriptive comparison, not a like-for-like measure of voter persuasion.

What the data can tell us

How this was built

County totals and candidate shares come from the connected NC-06 election comparison workbook. The interactive map combines precinct returns with the NC State Board of Elections August 24, 2026 precinct GIS file. DMA lines distinguish the Charlotte and Greensboro–High Point–Winston-Salem market portions within the district.

The website is a descriptive election analysis, not an independently audited official canvass.

Important caveats

Precinct-level returns in this workbook do not allocate all early, absentee and special reporting votes to home precincts. Therefore, precinct colors and geographic patterns may differ from a fully allocated voter-level map.

County-level 2024–2026 vote changes are raw totals, not turnout rates.

Source materials: NC-06 election comparison spreadsheet; NCSBE precinct GIS, August 2026. Prepared November 2026. This version excludes campaign planning projections and targets.
